An agent portal built around the agent's day

Thirteen example screens — the PRD's agent dashboard and commission fields, the sales pipeline, the doorstep record, lot availability and field capture — in the app's own design system, sky-blue as asked. Each screen carries a spec a builder can follow exactly: blocks in order, data, states, actions, and the PRD line it answers. Commission is shown as a shape with placeholders because the client has not fixed rates (open-questions.md:26) — nothing here invents a peso.

The recommendation, in one page

What this portal is for

An agent's day is not a web session. It is a phone, a bag of papers, a family at a table, and the office on the line. The portal exists to make three things true:

  • Nothing owed is forgotten. Today leads with a prioritized work list — overdue first, then due today, then waiting on the family — the grammar every serious sales tool uses.
  • The family is served in front of them. A prospect or client record opens on the doorstep with the call, the message, the history and the next action one thumb-tap away.
  • Money is told the truth about. Sales and commission are shown as a shape — per sale, per basis, per state — with placeholders until Villa configures its rates.

The ten decisions the design makes

  1. One Today screen, not a KPI dashboard. Figures carry meaning or they are not shown.
  2. The pipeline is lanes on desktop, cards on phones — phones get the sorted list, not a squeezed kanban.
  3. One primary action per screen; call/text is always at thumb height.
  4. Field capture works offline, queued on the device, and says so.
  5. Client records are deliberately shallower than staff records — the PRD's sensitive-data principle made visible.
  6. Appointments show confirmation state; nothing green until a human confirms.
  7. Lot availability uses the client's masterplan and the client's prices — never an invented lot or figure.
  8. Materials are the storefront's own pages, shared as trackable links — one true price for every agent.
  9. Commission is a shape, not a number, until the client decides the rules.
  10. Every screen is legible at 390 px: dominant headline, large plain words, big labelled controls.

The agent's day, and the jobs the portal must serve

Six jobs-to-be-done, each grounded in a source actually fetched for this review (full list and quotes in Provenance & sources).

1 · Start the day knowing the next move

"The work list helps sellers reach out to the next best lead or opportunity by displaying relevant sales information and customer context." Microsoft Learn, What is the Sales accelerator? (fetched 2026-09-16)

So Today leads with the next best action, not a KPI wall; records are sorted by priority and filtered to Today · Overdue · From tomorrow.

2 · Capture before the moment passes

"After you're out of a meeting, you can quickly add notes, create contacts, and update data in relevant records." Microsoft Learn, Dynamics 365 Sales mobile app overview (fetched 2026-09-16)

So New lead is one screen, one question at a time, with a photo and an offline queue.

3 · Open the client on the doorstep

"View and edit records, make calls … use the conversations inbox directly from your mobile device." HubSpot Knowledge Base, Install the HubSpot mobile app (updated Aug 14 2026; fetched 2026-09-16)

So the record page carries call, text, the history and the next action without a second tap.

4 · Move the pipeline forward on the move

"You can drag the opportunities to different lanes to move them from one status or stage to another. … The Kanban view is not supported in the mobile app." Microsoft Learn, Opportunity Kanban view (fetched 2026-09-16)

So phones get the sorted card list with one "Move to…" action; desktop keeps the lanes.

5 · Know what the work pays

"Use deal splits when multiple users contribute to a deal and each needs credit for a portion of the deal amount." HubSpot Knowledge Base, Split deal credit among users (fetched 2026-09-16)

"Earnings … detailed reports on earnings and payments … reconciliation details." Microsoft Learn, Partner Center earnings overview (fetched 2026-09-16)

So commission is a per-sale statement with basis, split and state — placeholder amounts until Villa configures its engine.

6 · Stay reachable where the signal is not

"You can use the Sales Hub app on Dynamics 365 for mobile in offline mode when you're experiencing intermittent connectivity." Microsoft Learn, Actions supported in online and offline mode (fetched 2026-09-16)

"Any on-screen element … should be large enough for reliable interaction … at least 48dp, separated by 8dp." Android Accessibility Help, Touch target size (fetched 2026-09-16)

So captures queue, reads are stamped, and controls are big and labelled — bigger than the 1 cm NNG floor because the agent is often moving.

Information architecture

Four groups, ten destinations. The current rail lists six flat staff nouns (Dashboard · Clients · Prospects · Applications · Sales & Commissions · Marketing Materials) and every page behind them is an honest "coming soon". This design keeps the routes that exist and adds the three the day needs (Appointments & tasks, Lot availability, New lead).

The plan, screen by screen — and what is deliberately left out

What is in

  • Today — the work list, the next action, the day's stops, the numbers.
  • Prospects + record — the pipeline and the doorstep view.
  • Clients + record — the after-sale book, permission-scoped.
  • Appointments & tasks — the seven PRD reasons, confirmation states, follow-ups.
  • Sales & commissions — statement shape, targets, clawbacks; placeholders.
  • Applications — where each one is stuck and whose move it is.
  • Lot availability — masterplan, real prices, share, the hold question.
  • Marketing & materials — the storefront's own pages as trackable shares.
  • New lead — offline capture from the field.
  • States & accessibility — the six PRD states plus offline and 403s.

What is deliberately left out (and why)

  • The full customer ledger — an agent serves the family, not the AR ageing report (roles-permissions.md:13).
  • Editing catalogue prices or plans — pricing is the office's; the agent quotes what the sheet says.
  • Managing other agents' books — a manager surface, not this portal.
  • Case operations (embalming status, dispatch, interment crew) — the agent's link is the family, not the operation.
  • A chat/social feed — the family talks to a human; the portal records what was said.
  • Ranking leaderboards as a default — the PRD's ranking analytics exists (§34) but the researched motivation model here is targets the office sets, not public shaming.
  • AI copilots — deterministic first (finance-billing.md:22); nothing in this portal needs a model.
  • Payments taken by the agent — collection is a real workflow with receipts and ORs; the portal links to the office until that contract exists.

States

  • Wrong password: 'That password doesn't match this email' — never an attempt counter, office number always shown.
  • Signed out: the same card with a plain 'you're signed out' sentence.
  • Service down: 'Sign-in is unavailable right now' + the office number.
  • Session expired mid-work: the portal returns here and says the work is kept (see States page).

The agent's phone is their terminal; sign-in must never be a wall. The card keeps the client's own blue and the review persona one tap away without ever inlining a password into public JS.

Page spec — a builder can follow this exactly

Purpose

Answer two questions before the agent's first call: what needs me today, and what do I do next?

Blocks, in order

  • Dominant headline: the date, a plain 'Good morning, Alex', and one sentence of truth ('3 follow-ups need you, first at 10:30').
  • One primary action band: the next best thing (the top overdue follow-up) with Call / Text / Log visit.
  • What needs you: work items in priority order — overdue first, then due today, then waiting on the family, then appointments.
  • Today's schedule: the next appointment with place, directions and what to bring.
  • Your numbers: pipeline value, sales this month, commission pending — each figure with its meaning and a target bar.
  • Quick actions: big labelled controls (New lead, Call the office, Lot availability, Share a price list).

Data — where it really comes from

  • Follow-ups/activities: crm-families (unbuilt) — fixtures carry inquiries only; the work-list shape is new.
  • New inquiries assigned to the agent exist today in lib/fixtures/crm/inquiries.json (Paolo Mendoza, assigned 'Alex Agent').
  • Appointments: booking-events-v1 (staff-side) + the scheduling fixture; agent-facing appointments are new.
  • Sales/commission: finance-billing.md:16–21 — commission engine not built; figures marked placeholders.

States

  • Nothing needs you: 'Nothing is overdue. Your first visit is 2:30 PM' + next appointment card.
  • Loading: skeleton lines keep the agent's name and the first action's space (no layout jump).
  • Error/offline: the list keeps the last read with a stamped time — 'showing what we read at 8:12 AM'.
  • Permission-denied (no orders scope): money cards show 'not included in your login — ask the office'.
  • Stale commission: 'last updated by the office on 15 Sep' rather than a silently old number.

Actions

Call / Text the next follow-up · Log a visit · Open the whole list · New lead

PRD line it answers

finance-billing.md:16–21 (agent dashboard fields: leads · prospects · follow-ups · sales · plans sold · lots sold · service/product sales · commission · collection · targets · conversion); commerce-catalog.md:33 (pipeline stages); facilities-scheduling.md:35 (appointment reasons).

Why this design

Every serious sales tool researched leads with a prioritized work list: Microsoft's work list puts 'the next-best customer' at the top, sorts by priority, filters by Today/Overdue, and removes the record when the activity completes. Dyn365's Sales mobile app is built around 'plan your day' and post-meeting capture. The design takes that grammar but adds the villa-specific truth: money that gates a service, and commission that cannot be invented.

Purpose

Prove the portal is designed for the agent's reality: one hand, bright sun, weak signal, and an office record that may be stale.

Blocks, in order

  • Empty states: no prospects, no clients, no appointments, no sales, nothing shared.
  • Loading: page skeleton, work-list skeleton, submitting state — the agent's name and the day never blank.
  • Offline & stale: last-read stamped content, queued captures, the exact sentence the agent can say to the family.
  • Error: 'We could not refresh... what we read at 8:12 AM' + the office number.
  • Permission-denied: the graceful 403 per block (money, commissions, client record) with who to ask.
  • Success: the two confirmations that matter — lead saved (even offline) and visit logged.
  • Accessibility: 48px+ targets (Android guidance), 16px base, labelled controls, status never by colour alone, reduced motion, contrast pairs.

Why this design

The field is the hard environment: sun, one hand, driving, no signal. NNG's touch research gives the floor (1cm targets; bigger when moving), Android's guidance 48dp with 8dp separation, and the offline standard is a stamped, honest last-read — never a bare spinner or a silent blank.

Questions only the captain / client can answer

Each is a genuine gate — a wrong guess would change the build, so the design holds a visible placeholder rather than a pretended answer.

  1. Who signs in — internal, external, or both? The PRD says the engine supports internal & external agents (finance-billing.md:17). Options: (a) both, with external agents seeing only their own book; (b) internal only first; (c) external agents get a lighter, separate surface. Recommendation: both, scoped to own book — the system already separates scopes; but external identity (TIN, contract, withholding) is real work.
  2. Which commission basis first? Fixed % · fixed ₱ · tiered · volume · milestone · split · multi-agent (finance-billing.md:17). Recommendation: one simple basis (fixed % with splits) to start, with the statement shape ready for the rest.
  3. Who sets targets, and does every agent see them? Options: monthly sales value only; collections included; targets hidden except to the agent themselves. Recommendation: monthly sales value per agent, visible to that agent and management.
  4. Can an agent reserve a lot in the field? The PRD's reservation flow has a period and expiry (memorial-property-gis.md:18). Options: agent can hold 24–72 h pending office confirmation; agent requests a hold only; no holds at all. Recommendation: request-a-hold, office confirms — the design shows this.
  5. What may a field agent see of a family's money and papers? Options: next due amount + date; full payment history; nothing (office answers). Recommendation: next due amount + date + receipt state, nothing about the rest.
  6. Phone-first PWA with offline capture? Options: yes (installable, queued captures); web-only. Recommendation: yes — the research and the market make the phone the field tool; offline capture is the only feature that cannot wait.
  7. Do agents submit applications and payments through the portal? Options: applications yes, payments no; both; neither (office records everything). Recommendation: applications yes (with office review), payments no.
  8. Which appointments may an agent book directly? Options: lot viewing at the park only; lot viewings + consultations; all seven reasons. Recommendation: lot viewing + planning consultation, confirmed by the office in v1.
  9. Is share tracking wanted, and is it comfortable for families? Options: per-link view counts (as designed); a single share link per material; no tracking. Recommendation: track opens, never track the family beyond the link — and say so in the app.
  10. Confirmation pass on this design. Options: approved as drawn / I have annotations. Unannotated = approved by default, as with the family portal.
