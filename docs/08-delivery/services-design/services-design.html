1. The page's job

“Help a grieving family find out what the funeral will cost and what happens next — then let them call, book a chapel stay, or send a request in the fewest possible steps.”

Nothing else. It is not a brochure, and no one arrives here to admire the layout. Every section either answers a question a family actually asks (what do I do first? how much is it? can I book the chapel?), or gets out of the way of one of the three actions: call, book, request.

The live page is 6,372 px tall on a desktop and 12,449 px on a phone, carries 98 interactive elements and 158 runs of text under 16 px. It is not missing content — it is missing order and legibility. The redesign keeps every figure and every option; it changes the sequence, the type, and where the actions sit.

2. Who reads it

A grieving family member

Often 45–75 years old, often reading on a phone, sometimes for the first time at 2 a.m. after a death, sometimes weeks later while comparing prices. Grief shortens working memory: one idea per block, short sentences, no jargon without the plain words right next to it.

Someone who has never arranged a funeral

Words like retrieval, interment, ORD coffin and a-la-carte mean nothing to them. The live page uses all four without a translation. The sample keeps the sheet's names (they appear on the contract) and adds one plain sentence under each.

A family on a small budget

They need to see the price of one service at a time, with the unit always visible (per service, per day, per stay, total), and to know what is optional. The sample never hides a price behind the booking flow and never shows an amount without its unit.

The sample's remaining 13–22 sub-16 px runs are the shared site header and footer links (the app's existing chrome, unchanged by this design); the page content itself has nothing below 16 px. “Controls under 44 px” counts buttons, inputs, selects and links styled as buttons — not inline sentence links.

The five specific failures

  1. The type is office-sized, not family-sized. Body 15 px; 184–192 text runs under 16 px (the smallest prose is 11.2 px); table headers 11.52 px; the lowest body contrast is 4.9:1 (AA for small text, not the AAA the captain asked for at body size).
  2. The chapel table cannot be used on a phone. 745 px wide in a 290 px box — the reader must scroll sideways, cell by cell, to compare one stay.
  3. Actions repeat inside cells. The chapel table alone carries 14 booking buttons and 7 request links; the at-need cards add 10 more. A family has to decide at every row whether they are booking or asking.
  4. There is no page navigation. The only sticky element is the site header. The seven shortcut chips at the top scroll away in the first screen, so a reader five screens down has no way back except the browser gesture.
  5. The first action isn't the one people need most. "Immediate assistance" opens the contact form; the 24/7 phone number sits in the header chip at 13 px and in the footer, never as the primary action of the page.

4. The recommendation

  1. Lead with the call, not the catalogue. A person looking for a funeral home at an hour of need wants a human. The hero states what the page is, and the panel directly under it is the 24/7 number with one sentence of what happens when they dial.
  2. Answer "what now?" before "how much?". Three numbered steps say who does what after the call; that is the reassurance that lets a family read prices calmly afterwards.
  3. One idea per block, 18 px body, ≥48 px targets. Senior-first type and spacing throughout this page (this is the captain's own accessibility ask), with no text under 16 px anywhere.
  4. Prices with their unit, in the same shape, everywhere. ₱2,500 per service · ₱9,000 for 5 days · ₱4,500 for 3 days · ₱1,500 per day. Never an amount without the unit.
  5. One row per decision. The chapel schedule becomes one row per stay length with a single Book button that carries the day count into the booking step; the full table survives behind a disclosure.
  6. Translate the sheet, keep its words. Each service card keeps the sheet's own name (it appears on the contract) and adds one plain sentence: "Retrieval" → "We bring your loved one into our care."
  7. Say what each button does. One legend per section: Add to cart = reserve now, pay nothing here; Request order = send the office a message, nothing is reserved. The live page never explains the pair.
  8. Make the page navigable. Breadcrumb at the top; a sticky "On this page" bar with five anchors and a back-to-top; every heading has its own anchor.
  9. Never leave the reader without a human. A navy help band closes the page, and on phones a small sticky call bar keeps the number visible the whole way down.
  10. Keep the trust block. The sheet provenance the live page already prints at the end stays, reworded to plain sentences and kept with the figures it explains.

6. Navigation

Breadcrumb

Home › Funeraria Memorial Services, above the H1, 16 px, with the current page not a link.

Sticky "On this page" bar

Five tapped anchors (What to do first · Services & prices · Embalming · Chapel dates · Where prices come from) plus Back to top. It sticks under the site header, and each anchor target carries scroll-margin-top so the heading is never hidden.

Back to top

Always present in the sticky bar on desktop; on phones the bar itself is a scrollable chip row, so the gesture stays in reach without a second floating button.

Human path

The header phone chip stays (existing chrome), the hero call button is primary, each price section repeats the number in one sentence, and phones get a sticky call bar. Nothing on the page is more than one tap from a person.

10. Accessibility standard

  • Target: WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere on the page, AAA for body copy where the palette allows. Measured body contrast in the sample: 7.83:1 minimum (live: 4.9:1).
  • Type: 18 px base, 1.6 line-height, 16 px smallest anywhere (live: 11.2 px kickers, 11.5 px table headers).
  • Targets: ≥48 px buttons on this page, ≥44 px for any control (live: 69 controls under 44 px at 390 px).
  • Structure: one H1; H2 per section; H3 inside; the schedule rows are a list, not a table, so a screen reader reads one decision; the sheet table keeps th/scope when it is open.
  • Motion: the skeleton shimmer stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Focus: the app's gold focus ring is untouched and every interactive element is a real link or button.
  • Language: sheet terms keep their names and gain a plain sentence; amounts always carry a unit.
