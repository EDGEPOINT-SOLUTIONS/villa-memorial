The recommendation, in one page

What the family portal is for. A family does not visit this product to manage an account. They arrive in the worst week of their lives, on a phone, from a group chat, in the middle of a wake — and they come back years later for a birthday. The portal has to work for both: an at-need week that is pure logistics and emotion, and a long, quiet relationship afterwards.

The main idea

One Home that answers "what needs me now", and an arrangement that is shared by the family — not an account summary. Today's dashboard describes the account; the family needs to know what to do next and who is with them.

The shape

Six groups, 15 pages, mobile-first. Our arrangement · Money & papers · Remembering · Getting help · Our family & privacy. Four pinned phone tabs plus More.

The stance

Grief-aware by rule, not by adjective. Plain words, no ledger-speak, no dark patterns, no marketing to a bereaved family, payment reminders that pause for a funeral, and a Privacy Center with a "who looked" log.

Today vs later

6 of 16 pages are mostly buildable on fixtures now (sign-in, Home hero, plans, payments aggregate, profile, states). The rest need the family contract — marked page by page, with the contract asks listed at the end.

Pinned: Home (what needs me), Case (the arrangement, the plan and the lot), Payments, Help (support, the coordinator's number, requests and appointments). Documents and Memorials sit in More and on Home — they matter intensely but less often than the four above.

Naming rules (small, load-bearing)

  • Never "My Funeral Cases" when there is one funeral: the page title uses the person's name — "Ernesto's funeral" — and the list view appears only when there is more than one.
  • "Memorial property" for lots, never "My Lots": families talk about the place, not the inventory.
  • Case numbers, invoice numbers and document numbers appear inside pages, never as page titles.
  • The word "case" appears only where the family already knows it (a paper they signed).

The dashboard: "what needs me now"

This is the logic the whole portal hangs on. Five bands, evaluated in this order, at most three rendered on a phone, one calm sentence when all five are empty.

Rules that decide the feed

RuleBehaviour
Trigger before dateSomething blocking the arrangement outranks anything with a due date, even a payment. A missing permit loses an interment slot; a payment can be rescheduled.
One action per cardEvery card has exactly one primary action and at most one quiet alternative ("Later", "Talk to us first").
Max threeOn a phone: three cards and "1 more thing". Never a wall of alerts.
The care windowFrom the death until two days after the interment, money cards are demoted below the schedule, lose the pre-selected "Pay now", and payment notifications are paused. If the family opens Payments, the truth is there in full.
Nothing vanishesA dismissed card returns if the condition is still true after 48 hours — but never more than once a day.
No streaks, no scoresNo badges, no progress gamification, no "complete your profile" prompts. The product never congratulates a family for using it.
Stale means stampedIf the data behind a card is older than an hour, the card says when it was last checked.
Nothing new is a state"Nothing needs you today" plus the next date is a designed state, not an empty feed.

The family journey this design serves

Every page in this artifact exists to serve one of these six moments. If a page serves none of them, it does not belong in the portal.

MomentWhat is actually happeningScreensWhat the design does about it
The callA death has just happened; a relative is calling at 2 AM.Support (duty line) · Sign-in invitationThe phone number is reachable without signing in; the invitation arrives by text minutes later.
The arrangementThe family sits with the director; decisions are made by several people.Home · Funeral case · Payments · DocumentsThe family circle joins with roles; the contract, the schedule and the balance are all visible.
The wake7–9 days of visitors, relatives flying in, questions repeated all day.Home (next service) · Family & access · SupportTimes in the visitor's own time zone; the live link for those who cannot travel; payment reminders stay quiet.
The intermentOne day with fixed times, a convoy, and a lot that must be settled.Home · Memorial property · DocumentsThe lot balance and the rule are stated early with a way out; permits and authorizations are tracked in the open.
AfterwardsThe paperwork, the receipts, the thanks — and a family that now has a place to visit.Documents · Memorials · RequestsMemorial draft, tributes moderated by the family, reminders that are opted into.
The years afterA birthday, an anniversary, All Souls', a marker that needs cleaning.Memorials · Remembrances · RequestsA page that is still there, a candle that can be lit, and one tap to ask for maintenance.

What it is for

Get a family member in fast, and make joining from an invitation obvious. Families arrive here from a text or email in the middle of the worst week of their lives — this screen has one job and no upsell.

Blocks, in order

  1. Left column (desktop only)Reassurance: what the portal holds, privacy promise, the park's name. Hidden on a phone — the phone goes straight to the form.
  2. The cardEmail → password → Keep me signed in → Sign in.
  3. Second door"Email me a sign-in link" — no password to remember for a relative who signs in once.
  4. Invitation noteOne plain sentence explaining the invite link (the normal way family joins).

Data it shows

Field / objectWhere it comes from
Accountidentity-access: email + password, server-side httpOnly session (works today)
Invitation tokenNEW — identity-access invitation: family_id, invited_by, role, expiry, single-use

States

  • DefaultAs shown.
  • Wrong password"That password doesn't match this email." + Try again · Email me a link. Never counts attempts in the copy, never locks without a phone number to call.
  • Invitation expiredExplains in one line + "Ask your coordinator to send a new invite" with their number.
  • Service down"Sign-in is having a moment. Please try again, or call the office." The office number is always visible.
  • Signed out"You've been signed out to keep your family's records safe."
Why this design: Families do not choose to use this product, grief does. The screen is calm, single-purpose, and keeps a human exit (call the office) visible at every failure point.

States

Language

  • Never ledger-speak. Banned from the family surface: AR aging, 120+, delinquency, default, forfeiture, applicant, ticket status ASSIGNED, utilisation, KPI. Used instead: left to pay, past due, we can arrange it, the arrangement, your request, who is handling it.

  • No exclamation marks, no "Congratulations". Positive states are quiet: "Settled — thank you."
  • Every failure has a human. Every error state offers a phone number.

No dark patterns — the list we hold ourselves to

  • No countdown timers, no scarcity ("3 families viewing this lot"), no urgency colour on anything that is not genuinely urgent.
  • No pre-ticked consent, ever — including for memorials and reminders; marketing defaults off.
  • No cancellation maze: closing an account or deleting a memorial is one screen with a clear explanation of what happens next.
  • No "are you sure you want to leave?" guilt prompts, no email-address begging.
  • No upsell inside an at-need flow. Add-ons are offered once, in the arrangement, by a person.
  • Payments are never pre-selected as the primary action during the care window.

Calm pacing & dignified imagery

  • One primary action per screen; the page does not compete with itself.
  • Motion is 120–200 ms and fully disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • A loved one's photo is never required: an empty frame with a monogram is a respectful default, and the family adds a photo when they are ready.
  • No stock faces, no caskets or graves as decoration, no "success" illustrations. The only photography is the family's own and the client's own park imagery.
  • Budget imagery: the client's park and lot photographs, used once per page at most, always captioned.

Open questions for the captain

Each answer queues back to the worker, and every answer lands in the report. Annotate any page in place instead if the question is easier answered there. Anything left unanswered is recorded as an unresolved captain decision — nothing is closed silently.

Q1

One Home instead of two dashboards?

The PRD lists a Customer Dashboard and a Family Dashboard as separate screens. This design recommends a single Home that leads with the shared arrangement and shows who else is in it — the split adds a decision the family should never have to make.

Recommend one Home.
Q2

Which four tabs stay pinned on a phone?

Mobile-first means four tabs and a More. The recommendation is Home · Case · Payments · Help — the four things a family reaches for during an arrangement.

Recommend Home · Case · Payments · Help.
Q3

The care window: pause money nudges during a funeral?

Between the death and two days after the interment, the design demotes money cards below the schedule and pauses payment notifications. The family can still open Payments and see everything. This is a product rule, so it needs your call.

Recommend the 2-day rule.
Q4

Show the 'no interment until fully paid' rule to the family?

Both Villa agreements say a lot must be fully paid before an interment (2025 and 2026 versions). Today a family discovers this on the day. The design shows it on Home and Memorial property with three ways out and the coordinator's number.

Recommend showing it early with options.
Q5

Memorial scope for the first release

The digital memorial is the biggest build and the biggest differentiator. The recommendation is to ship the family-kept page (story, photos, tributes with family moderation, visibility levels) first, and leave the live wake tools for the next phase.

Recommend page + tributes first.
Q6

Abuloy (contributions): whose money is it?

The cultural module lists five money models and says not to assume the funeral home holds the funds. This is a client decision before any UI ships.

Recommendation: start with 'family's own account' (informational) — no liability, no reconciliation.
Q7

Languages: which ones, and when?

Most families here speak Chavacano or Filipino at home. The design ships the language switch and English copy first, with short sentences that translate without re-layout.

Recommend English + Filipino first, Chavacano next.
Q8

Can the public search for a loved one's memorial?

'Find my loved one' and public memorial search are in the PRD, and the privacy rules are an open client question (docs/07-client-villa/open-questions.md). This design defaults to the narrowest safe option.

Recommend link-by-default, per-memorial opt-in.
Q9

Show the family who on staff looked at their records?

The Privacy Center's 'who looked' log is the strongest trust feature in this design, and it needs an audit projection. It also changes how staff behave around sensitive records.

Recommend the full log.
Q10

Confirmation pass: anything in the pages to change?

Everything else in this artifact is a design recommendation the report records. If a page is wrong, annotate it — or queue this and name the page. No answer here means 'approved as drawn'.

Anything unannotated is recorded as approved by default.
